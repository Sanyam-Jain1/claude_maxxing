Research note · Indian power exchange microstructure · 12 Nov 2026

The first 15 minutes of every hour clear cheaper in India's real-time power market

On IEX's Real-Time Market, the block starting at :00 clears about ₹0.10/kWh below what its neighbours imply. This holds in every year from 2022 to 2025 and against the :15 block sold in the same auction. I could find no published description of it.

Claim. For IEX RTM, define the local dip of block j as Dj = pj − (pj−1 + pj+1)/2. Averaged over all hours, D at :00 minus D at :30 is −0.108 ₹/kWh (95% CI −0.117 to −0.100, day-bootstrap, Jan 2023 – Sep 2025), about 2–3% of the average price. The Day-Ahead Market shows the same sign at 40% of the size, and it has nearly disappeared there in 2025.

Falsified if the same statistic on an independent RTM sample (Oct 2025 onward, or PXIL/HPX) is not negative with a magnitude of at least 0.03 ₹/kWh.

Research question

India's exchanges trade 96 fifteen-minute blocks per day and have no hourly product. Do prices nevertheless carry an hour-periodic structure, and if so, is it the European "sawtooth" under another name or something different?

Data

Two public GitHub datasets of IEX market snapshots, collected by different people. I had no access to your own data or to IEX directly (the sandbox blocks most hosts).

  • A: DAM, GDAM and RTM, Jan 2023 – Sep 2025, 1,005 days, price, cleared volume, buy and sell bid volume per block.
  • B: RTM only, Apr 2022 – Jun 2025, 1,181 days, with session IDs.

On the 912 overlapping days, RTM prices differ by more than ₹0.001 in 0.1% of blocks. So the two are effectively one source (IEX) transcribed twice; B adds nine earlier months.

Path to the hypothesis

Step 1. Prices do move on an hourly and half-hourly lattice Measured

RTM's half-hour steps are expected: each auction has newer information than the last. The hour-boundary excess in DAM is not, since all 96 blocks clear at once. DAM buy and sell bid volumes show the same thing (hour-boundary changes 1.7–2× the within-hour ones), so part of the bid stack is submitted in hour-flat shapes.

Step 2. The German sawtooth hypothesis fails Rejected

If hour-flat day-ahead positions left a ramp-shaped residual for RTM, the within-hour pattern should reverse between rising and falling hours. It does not. Detrended RTM price at :00 is −0.075 in rising-price hours and −0.091 in falling-price hours. A regression of the :00 residual on the local price trend, the RTM bid trend, the DAM volume step at the boundary and the DAM :00 residual leaves the intercept at −0.073 (se 0.003) with R² of 3%.

Step 3. What remains is an unconditional dip at :00 Measured

A one-block dip of depth δ should give second differences of (−δ, +δ/2, 0, +δ/2) at (:00, :15, :30, :45). Observed for RTM:

The same-auction comparison matters most. Blocks :00 and :15 are bid at the same moment with the same information, so the gap cannot come from news arriving between auctions. The :30/:45 pair, which shares that structure, shows nothing.

Evidence against, and limits

  • The median block-level gap between :00 and :15 is zero to four decimals. The mean comes from a minority of hours with large gaps, concentrated at 05:00–07:00, 10:00, 13:00 and 16:00–18:00.
  • Two hours go the other way: 19:00 (+0.14) and 09:00 (+0.06). 19:00 is positive in DAM and GDAM as well. A single "load dips at the hour" story does not cover this.
  • The effect is shrinking by roughly a fifth to a quarter per year in RTM and is close to gone in DAM. It may not survive.
  • Both datasets come from IEX's website. An error on IEX's side, or a mislabelled block convention, would affect both. Dataset B labels blocks as ranges ("00:00-00:15"), which fixes the convention.
  • All prices are national unconstrained clearing prices. Area prices under congestion were not available.

Mechanism

Deduction The bid volumes say what happens: at :00 buyers ask for less and sellers offer more, most strongly at 06:00, 13:00 and 18:00. They do not say why.

Hypothesis Scheduled supply and switchable load change at the top of the hour while the rest of demand moves smoothly. Candidates are hour-blocked bilateral and term-ahead contracts, hydro peaking schedules, industrial shift and lunch breaks (13:00), and feeder changeovers in which one group is switched off on the hour and the next picks up over several minutes. Each leaves utilities briefly long in the first block. The same imbalance would produce the hour-boundary frequency excursions that CERC has documented. I have not verified this link.

The standard European mechanism (stepped schedules against a smooth ramp) predicts a sign that follows the ramp. That was tested in step 2 and rejected, so if the Indian frequency phenomenon and this price dip share a cause, it is closer to a recurring one-sided surplus than to a symmetric ramp mismatch.

Simplest tests

  1. Replication, ten minutes. Run scripts/secdiff.py on your own IEX RTM history, especially Oct 2025 onward. Prediction: negative, between −0.03 and −0.09 ₹/kWh, with the hour-of-day profile correlating above 0.6 with the chart here.
  2. Mechanism, one afternoon. Take Grid-India frequency data at one-minute or finer resolution. For each hour compute mean frequency in minutes 0–15 minus the mean of minutes −15–0 and 15–30. Prediction: positive on average, and across the 24 hours negatively correlated with the bars in the chart, with 19:00 reversed or flat. A null result kills the shared-cause hypothesis but not the price finding.
  3. Other venues. PXIL and HPX real-time prices should show the same sign if the cause is physical and not specific to IEX's bidders.
  4. Bid curves. IEX publishes aggregate demand and supply curves per block. Comparing :00 with :15 in the same session shows whether the shift is in price-insensitive volume (schedules) or in priced bids.

What this is worth in practice

  • A buyer with 15 minutes of flexibility pays about ₹0.10/kWh less at :00 than at :15, with no forecasting. Equal-weighted, the :00 blocks average ₹4.52 against ₹4.59 for all blocks.
  • A seller, including a discharging battery, should prefer any block other than :00.
  • It is not a standalone battery trade. A 10% round-trip loss at ₹4.6 costs about ₹0.46, more than four times the gap. It is a tie-breaker for which block to charge in.
  • Price forecasts for RTM should carry a quarter-of-hour term that varies by hour. Models with only hour-of-day features will be biased by up to ₹0.4 at 18:00.
  • These figures ignore the price impact of acting on them. If it is a thin surplus, it will not absorb much volume.
